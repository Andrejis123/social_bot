## The opening

DPB (Dopravný podnik Bratislava, city-owned public transport operator) lost its
entire marketing department, who quit in protest. Andy knows this via a private
inside contact.

**Do NOT reference the resignations, the protest, or any inside knowledge in any
outreach.** It burns the source and reads as vulture behaviour. Open generically:
"this is what I do, we do it well, I think I can help you." The trigger event is
timing intelligence for us only, never a talking point.

**Never frame the offer as cheaper-than-headcount to DPB.** It is a city-owned
body in a live labour dispute. Replacement-cost maths makes whoever signs it the
next paragraph in a political story, which is exactly why they would not sign.
Correct frame: interim benchmarking support WHILE THEY REBUILD the team, an input
for whoever remains and for the agency they will hire. Andy's employer-cost
figures (~EUR 1800-1900 gross starting salary, ~EUR 2400/mo true employer cost)
are for our own pricing confidence, kept out of the pitch.

## Why the vertical is the real prize, not the single deal

Transit operators are close to an ideal fit:
- Their "competitors" are peer agencies with zero reason to hide from each other.
  Transit marketing teams already openly copy each other, so no competitive-
  secrecy friction and no awkwardness about where the data came from.
- They are numerous, directly comparable, and publicly funded, so budgets recur.
- **One scraped dataset serves every buyer in the vertical.** Scrape ~12 European
  operators once and sell DPB, DPMK Košice, DPMŽ Žilina, DPP Praha, and Polish
  and Hungarian operators off the same pipeline. Marginal cost per extra client
  is near zero. This is a different business shape from one-client-one-scrape and
  is the main reason to take it seriously.

## Product shape

Peers to benchmark: CZ/PL/HU/AT weighted (DPP Praha, MPK Kraków, BKK Budapest,
Wiener Linien is the acknowledged best-in-class), plus a wider European tail.

**Stories are in scope, not just backfill (Andy, 07-08).** Historical posts are
available immediately via HikerAPI so a first sample deck needs no waiting
period, but story capture is our actual differentiator and must run live from
the start. Backfill-only would sell the weaker half of the product.

Positioning caution: "copy what other agencies do" is weak, since anyone can open
Instagram for free. Lead instead on what is genuinely hard by hand: systematic
monthly capture across ~10 cities, engagement data attached, categorised by
campaign type, including stories that vanish in 24h. Say plainly that a report is
ammunition, not creative output. Overpromising there loses month two.

## Pricing and procurement

Andy's anchor: EUR 500/mo, framed as ~EUR 200-600/mo and written off as a business
expense. EUR 500/mo = EUR 6,000/yr, which should sit under Slovakia's low-value
procurement threshold and avoid a public tender. **Confirm the actual threshold
before pricing** - it, not willingness to pay, is what caps the price. Going over
it turns a phone call into a tender we would likely lose.

## Blocker (agreed): legal entity

A municipal company cannot pay an individual with no entity. Needs invoice, VAT
position, contract, and near-certainly a DPA. Andy is an Irish tax resident who
summers in Slovakia, registered with Revenue, holds no živnosť. This is being
split into its own dedicated project covering tax, finance and legality across
all his projects. See `research/entity_finance_handoff.md`. The GDPR paperwork pack task in
Notion is a prerequisite here too.

Sequencing: entity and paperwork FIRST, or the pitch lands and cannot be invoiced.
